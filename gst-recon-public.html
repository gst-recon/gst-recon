GST Recon is a free client-side GST toolkit for Indian businesses and accountants. It reconciles GSTR-2B and GSTR-1 against your books, validates GSTINs in bulk, estimates GSTR-3B payable, and helps you track business finances and receivables. Frequently asked questions

Is my data uploaded anywhere?

No. Your register and portal files are read and reconciled inside your browser and are never uploaded to a server. Finance entries and reconciliation history are stored only in this browser's local storage, on your device.

Do I need a GSTN or GST portal login?

No. You download GSTR-2B / GSTR-1 files from the portal yourself and upload them here — GST Recon never asks for your GST portal credentials.

Is the GSTR-3B summary a return filing?

No — it is an estimate helper only. It does not file anything and does not cover reverse charge, late fee or interest. Verify the figures with your CA before filing.

Is Google sign-in required?

The tools need a free account. You can sign in with Google, or create an account with your name, email and a password — both work the same way.

Which file formats work?

.xlsx, .xls, .csv and .json files, up to 10 MB each. JSON files must be an array of invoice objects — jaise [{"Supplier GSTIN": "27…", "Invoice No": "INV-001", "Taxable Value": 10000, …}]. Column names are matched flexibly — the accepted names are listed above the upload boxes in each reconciliation tab. Chhoti-moti file problems auto-fix ho jaati hain: blank rows skip hoti hain, header row file me jahan bhi ho dhoondh li jaati hai, semicolon wali CSV aur alag-alag date formats (DD/MM/YYYY, YYYY-MM-DD, 05-Oct-2026) chal jaate hain, aur koi tax column missing ho to uski value 0 maan kar aapko note dikhaya jaata hai — sirf GSTIN ya Invoice No column hi sach me zaroori hai.

Where is my reconciliation history stored?

In this browser (local storage), so the Dashboard can show your totals and recent runs. Clearing this site's browser data deletes it — download the Excel reports to keep permanent copies.

Haan, 100% free. Vendor Master me aap apne vendors ke GSTIN, naam, trade name, address aur status ek baar save karte hain. 📚 GST & Tax Guide

GST kya hai, kyun bharte hain, kab bharte hain — and what TDS returns are and when they are filed. Simple, standard information for Indian businesses and accountants.

General information only (last reviewed Nov 2026) — always verify against current CBIC / Income-tax notifications. For tax advice, consult your CA.

What is GST and why do we pay it?

GST (Goods and Services Tax) is a destination-based indirect tax on the supply of goods and services. It was launched on 1 July 2017 and replaced many older indirect taxes — such as excise duty, VAT, service tax and octroi — with one single tax.

Why GST? It creates a single national market, and its input tax credit chain removes tax-on-tax (cascading), so tax is charged only on the value added at each stage. It also brings wider, more uniform compliance across India.

GST is paid by registered businesses. They collect it from their customers on sales and deposit it with the government, after adjusting the tax they already paid on their purchases.

Who must register?

Generally, registration is required when turnover crosses:

  • ₹40 lakh — for suppliers of goods
  • ₹20 lakh — for suppliers of services

Lower limits apply in special-category states. Registration is also mandatory in some cases regardless of turnover — for example, inter-state supply, e-commerce sellers, persons liable under reverse charge, and casual taxable persons.

What is ITC?

ITC (Input Tax Credit) means the tax you paid on your purchases can be set off against the tax payable on your sales — so you pay only the difference to the government.

GSTR-2B shows the eligible ITC as per your suppliers' filings. Only matched and eligible ITC should be claimed, which is why reconciling your Purchase Register with GSTR-2B matters.

TDS under GST (Section 51)

Specified persons — such as government departments, local authorities and notified bodies — must deduct TDS under GST when they make payments to suppliers.

  • Rate: 2% — that is 1% CGST + 1% SGST, or 2% IGST
  • When: on payments exceeding ₹2.5 lakh under a contract (value excluding GST)
  • Deposit & return: via GSTR-7 by the 10th of the next month
  • For the deductee: the amount appears as credit, and a GSTR-7A certificate is generated

TCS under GST (Section 52)

E-commerce operators collect TCS (Tax Collected at Source) at 1% on the net taxable supplies made through their platform by other sellers.

They report and deposit it in GSTR-8 by the 10th of the next month.

TDS under Income Tax

TDS (Tax Deducted at Source) under Income Tax is tax deducted at the time of payment itself — on payments like salary, interest, contractor payments, rent and professional fees. The payer deducts it, deposits it with the government, and the payee gets credit for it.

TDS returns — what and when:

  • Returns: Form 24Q (salary TDS) and Form 26Q (non-salary TDS), filed quarterly
  • Due dates: Q1 (Apr–Jun) — 31 Jul · Q2 (Jul–Sep) — 31 Oct · Q3 (Oct–Dec) — 31 Jan · Q4 (Jan–Mar) — 31 May
  • Payment: by the 7th of the next month (30 April for March deductions), via Challan 281
  • Certificates: Form 16 (salary, annual) and Form 16A (quarterly)

Late fee & interest (GST)

Filing a GST return late attracts a late fee of ₹50 per day (₹20 per day for nil returns), subject to maximum caps.

Delayed tax payment also attracts interest at 18% p.a. on the delayed tax amount.

Note: verify the current caps — they vary by return and are updated through notifications.

How matching works

A row counts as matched only when GSTIN, invoice number, invoice date, taxable value, CGST, SGST and IGST are all exactly equal in both files. Everything else lands in one of these buckets:

Missing in the portal file

In your books but not in GSTR-2B or GSTR-1 — for purchases, ITC you may not be able to claim yet; for sales, invoices you still need to file.

Extra in the portal file

In GSTR-2B or GSTR-1 but not in your books — entries you may have missed recording.

Value & tax mismatches

Same GSTIN and invoice number in both files, but the taxable value or the CGST / SGST / IGST amounts differ.

Duplicates & invalid GSTINs

Same invoice entered twice in one file, and GSTINs that fail the official check-digit validation — each with the exact reason.
